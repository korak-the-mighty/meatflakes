# Meatflakes — first expert conversation brief

**Purpose:** request a feasibility conversation, not a process quote based on a fixed recipe. The product is a concept, with no validated formulation or shelf life.

## Desired user experience

- **Format:** cooked real meat in visible, discrete flakes or pieces; not a powder, a jerky snack or conventional fine floss.
- **Use:** shake or pour from a pack onto ordinary rice, pasta, ramen, eggs, potatoes or salad; ideally enjoyable directly from the pack. Hot food is a use occasion, not a presumed safety step.
- **Texture:** dry crispness is attractive; pleasant softening in hot or moist dishes is another target. We need to learn if both are possible in one SKU.
- **Meats:** start with chicken; evaluate beef and pork. A fatty guanciale-inspired carbonara version is an important stretch case.
- **Storage:** unopened ambient pantry storage is the ambition. **12–24+ months is a target to investigate, not an achieved or promised shelf life.** Post-opening instructions also need study.

## Decisions we need your help to frame

1. Which process families might safely deliver recognizable meat flakes at pilot and industrial scale? Compare hot-air, vacuum and freeze drying only if relevant; please propose better routes if appropriate.
2. What pathogen-control and post-process hygiene design would be required for the exact intended ready-to-eat use? Which analyses and regulatory classification must be resolved first?
3. How should finished water activity, moisture distribution, fat oxidation, texture, rehydration and package oxygen/moisture barrier be tested? What differs for guanciale?
4. What would an initial feasibility study cost and deliver? Which failure criteria would let us stop early?
5. Which partner capabilities are in-house, and who would do independent safety, shelf-life, packaging and regulatory work?

**Available now:** a concept, candidate use cases and a planned immediate sensory trial. **Not available:** pilot data, measured water activity, microbial results, shelf-life evidence, a manufacturing quote or approved claims.

The first call should end with a written outline of the cheapest credible next experiment and the evidence it would produce. See [food science](../03_RESEARCH/FOOD_SCIENCE.md), [shelf life](../03_RESEARCH/SHELF_LIFE.md) and [go/no-go gates](../05_BUSINESS/GO_NO_GO.md).
