## Per pouch and per useful meal

Test a **40–60 g pouch as a working size range**, not a decided pack. Measure actual grams people use to make a dish feel more like a meal; then compute servings per pouch. A cheaper cost per gram is irrelevant if customers need too much for the promised effect.

| Measure | Formula / question |
| --- | --- |
| Net revenue per pouch | Retail price excluding applicable tax and channel deductions |
| Gross profit per pouch | Net revenue minus fully loaded variable cost |
| Gross margin | Gross profit / net revenue |
| Cost per useful serving | Fully loaded pouch cost / observed useful servings |
| Contribution after acquisition / distribution | Gross profit minus channel-specific costs |

For retail, obtain actual retailer/distributor terms; for direct sales, include fulfillment, payment and customer acquisition. Compare the resulting **price per useful meal** with bacon bits, floss, fresh/cooked meat and other convenient protein additions in the launch market. Do not assume a 40–60 g pouch contains several satisfying servings until the consumer test establishes actual use mass.

## Decision sensitivity

Vary finished yield, pack size, usage per meal, packaging barrier cost, rejection rate and channel margin. Note the combination at which gross margin stops supporting the business. An expensive guanciale pack could be viable as a culinary finish while failing the broader “make a meal” promise; treat these as distinct propositions.
