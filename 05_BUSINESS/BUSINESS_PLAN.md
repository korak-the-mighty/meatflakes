# Business hypotheses

## Jobs and potential customers

| Segment to interview | Situation | Unproven value proposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Busy home cooks | Plain pasta, rice, noodles, or potatoes | A pantry addition makes the dish feel more substantial |
| Small households | Fresh meat pack is inconvenient for one meal | Portion control and less unused meat |
| Foodservice / meal-kit teams | Need consistent toppings and preparation time | A repeatable meat finish with simple handling |

These are interview hypotheses, not measured segments. Research must determine who buys, when, and whether the meat amount is enough to justify “meal” positioning.

## Revenue and channel options to test

- **Retail pantry packs:** test shelf placement, trial conversion, repeat purchase, and post-opening use.
- **Single-serve sachets:** test portion satisfaction, cost, and packaging burden.
- **Foodservice ingredient:** test preparation speed, operational fit, and minimum order economics.

Do not forecast sales from the size of the general meat or seasoning market. Relevant demand is the number of occasions where buyers prefer this exact convenience, format, taste, and price over available alternatives.

## Unit-economics worksheet

For each candidate, record raw meat cost and yield after cooking/drying; seasoning and other ingredients; labor; energy; co-manufacturing; primary and secondary packaging; quality assurance and laboratory testing; spoilage and returns; freight; channel margin; and VAT. The resulting cost per *actual serving* must be compared with an acceptable consumer price and with fresh meat, bacon bits, and meat floss. No values have been established. Use the [unit-economics worksheet](UNIT_ECONOMICS.md) to collect real numbers.

## Main commercial risks

- Lean meat may become too dry or expensive per usable serving.
- Fatty guanciale may need a process or pack that raises cost or shortens quality life.
- Existing bacon bits and meat floss may already solve enough of the job.
- Consumers may perceive a small topping as garnish rather than meal-making meat.
- Species-specific facilities, labeling, and logistics may complicate a multi-meat range.

## Evidence gates

Advance only when a defined target customer repeatedly uses a safe prototype, prefers it in at least one real dish, accepts its intended price, and the fully loaded economics can support the chosen channel. See [validation plan](../07_TESTING/VALIDATION_PLAN.md) and [market notes](../03_RESEARCH/MARKET.md).
