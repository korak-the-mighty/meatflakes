# Brand working notes

**Meatflakes** is the working name, not a cleared trademark. A trademark search and consumer name test remain open.

## Promise to test

“Real meat, ready to finish the meal.” This expresses the intended use without asserting an unverified nutrition, health, shelf-life, or safety outcome. If testing shows the product is mainly a flavor garnish, revise the promise rather than claiming it makes a meal.

## Visual and verbal rules

- Show actual pieces of the finished product on ordinary food. Avoid imagery that implies a piece size or meat content the product does not deliver.
- Use plain species names and disclose ingredients accurately. “Guanciale” must correspond to what the final ingredient actually is and to the applicable labeling rules.
- Describe texture specifically: “crunchy,” “softens in hot food,” or neither, based on tests for the exact SKU.
- Do not call the product fresh, unprocessed, preservative-free, high-protein, or shelf-stable without substantiation and labeling review.
- Keep comparisons respectful. Meat floss, jerky, and bacon bits are established categories and important benchmarks.

## Possible positioning routes — hypotheses

| Route | Consumer idea | What would support it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeknight helper | Finish pasta or rice in seconds | Repeated use in home meal diaries |
| Pantry protein | Keep a real-meat topping on hand | Verified nutrition per realistic serving and safe ambient shelf life |
| Culinary finish | A distinctive final touch, especially guanciale on carbonara | Blind preference over existing toppings and a credible price premium |

Choose a route only after product, consumer, and labeling evidence agree. Packaging copy and design have not been developed.
