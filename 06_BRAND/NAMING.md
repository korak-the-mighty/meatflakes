# Naming and domains

**Meatflakes** is the working project and category name. The earlier conversation says domains were secured, but this repository has **no verified inventory of domains, registrant, expiry dates or trademark clearance**. Record those facts privately before any brand commitment; do not infer rights from a domain purchase.

## Name test

Ask target customers, without explaining first: What product do you expect from “Meatflakes”? Is it appetizing, clear and easy to remember? Does it sound like real meat, cereal, pet food or a seasoning? Compare against two or three candidate names only after the product format is defined. Test pronunciation and comprehension in the intended launch language.

## Clearance work

Before packaging or paid marketing, search relevant trademark registers and classes in intended countries, inspect similar food names, confirm domain ownership and check social handles. A qualified trademark professional should review material conflicts. A descriptive product name may also need a more distinctive brand layer; that is a hypothesis, not a legal conclusion.

Keep the name separate from product claims: a catchy word cannot imply proven safety, protein content or shelf life. See [brand territory](BRAND.md) and [IP strategy](../04_IP_PATENT/IP_STRATEGY.md).
