# Manufacturing exploration

This is a **pilot-partner brief**, not a recipe or safe processing instruction. Meat processing must be designed and validated by an appropriately equipped and approved food business.

## Candidate process map to investigate

1. Select traceable meat inputs and define species-specific specifications.
2. Prepare and shape recognizable pieces; document cut and size distribution.
3. Apply a professionally validated pathogen-control step appropriate to the actual meat and process.
4. Form or dry the product toward the selected texture and measured stability targets, while controlling recontamination.
5. Cool, handle, and package under appropriate hygiene controls.
6. Release only lots meeting validated process, microbiological, packaging, and quality criteria.

The order and nature of these steps may change after expert hazard analysis. Drying alone is not a presumed kill step; see [USDA FSIS jerky safety guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/meat-fish/jerky).

## Equipment and partner questions

- Which facility has approval and experience for the intended species and processed meat category under [EU Regulation 853/2004](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX%3A32004R0853)?
- What process can produce repeatable flake size and meat appearance without turning the product into powder or floss?
- How are uneven pieces, fat-rich pockets, and post-process contamination identified and controlled?
- Which packs protect from oxygen, light, and moisture while remaining convenient for intended use?
- What are actual yield, cycle time, cleaning/changeover costs, and minimum economic batch size for each species?
- Can guanciale retain its intended culinary character through a process that also meets safety and shelf-life requirements?

## Pilot batch record fields

Supplier and lot, meat cut and composition, pretreatment, time and temperature records, process settings, yield, piece-size distribution, finished water activity, microbiological results, package specification and seal integrity, storage conditions, sensory results, deviations, and disposition. Keep raw measurements; do not replace them with “passed” alone.

US [FSIS ready-to-eat shelf-stable jerky HACCP model](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/guidelines/2021-0004) can help frame questions, but the actual EU facility and product need their own legal and scientific validation.
