# IP and patent questions

**Public document — 5 October 2026.** The repository owner chose to publish this concept in a public GitHub repository. Public disclosure can affect patent options; an IP professional should assess the actual publication history, jurisdictions, and any proposed filing. Nothing in this repository is a patent application, patentability opinion, or freedom-to-operate opinion.

## Concept disclosed here

The proposed product is shelf-stable, visible real-meat flakes or toppings for ordinary meals, including chicken, beef, and pork or guanciale; crunchy or softening behavior is under investigation; carbonara with guanciale flakes is a signature example. See [CORE.md](CORE.md). No proprietary process, validated formulation, or experimental result is asserted.

## Known adjacent publications

- [US4781937A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4781937A/en) describes cooked dehydrated, rehydratable animal-and-vegetable food in flake or powder form. Its composition and process differ from the intended meat-visible concept in some respects, but claim comparison has not been performed.
- [CN103431416A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103431416A/en) describes a crisp meat-floss process. The translated abstract is a search lead, not a reliable claim interpretation.
- [CN103622045A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103622045A/en) describes dried meat floss production and discusses processing and storage issues.
- Commercial [real-bacon bits](https://www.hormel.com/Brands/Real-Bacon/Hormel-Bacon-Toppings/Hormel-Real-Bacon-Bits-6oz?pr_rd_page=8) and [pork floss](https://order.beechenghiang.com.sg/pub/CNY26%20BCH%20Corporate%20Price%20List.pdf) are non-patent prior-art leads.

The [EPO explains that novelty is assessed against what was made available to the public](https://link.epo.org/web/legal/guidelines-epc/en-epc-guidelines-2026-hyperlinked.pdf); [WIPO's international search guidance treats public Internet disclosure as prior art](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/ispe/11_12_22). Those principles are reasons to document publication dates and get jurisdiction-specific advice, not conclusions about any claim.

## Questions for patent counsel

1. Which exact product or process features, if any, are technically new and non-obvious after a professional prior-art search?
2. What has already been disclosed publicly, including this repository and earlier conversations or demonstrations, and when?
3. Are there process, composition, packaging, or use claims worth investigating? What evidence and prototypes would be needed?
4. Which active claims could affect manufacture and sale in target countries? Patentability and freedom to operate are different analyses.
5. What should be kept as know-how, and what brand names or designs require separate searches?

Do not infer that a patent citation either blocks or permits a product. See [research/patents.md](research/patents.md) for search terms and limitations.
