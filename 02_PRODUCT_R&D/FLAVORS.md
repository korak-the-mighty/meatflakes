# Flavor and species directions

**Candidate concepts only.** None is a launched SKU or proven stable formulation. Start with plain, meat-forward variants so the eating experience is not concealed by seasoning.

| Direction | Proposed role | Dishes to test | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roast chicken | Universal first product and Prototype 001 base | Rice, noodles, potato, eggs, salad | Dryness, chicken recognition, cost/yield |
| Beef / smoky beef | Hearty alternative | Potato, rice, noodles | Chew, dried flavor, rehydration |
| Pork | Savory everyday variant | Eggs, rice, pasta | Existing bacon-bit and floss competition |
| Guanciale / carbonara | Distinctive culinary use case and technical stretch goal | Carbonara first, then other pasta | Fat release, oxidation, pack life, accurate naming |

The carbonara test should compare cooked guanciale, candidate flakes, a relevant commercial bacon topping and a no-meat control in the same base recipe. The question is whether a pantry format creates a satisfying, credible use case. It may need a different process and package from lean chicken.

Do not claim “guanciale” on a final label unless the ingredient and naming comply with the target market's rules. Seasoning variants such as pepper, chili or herbs can follow only after the base meat and texture are worth using repeatedly.
