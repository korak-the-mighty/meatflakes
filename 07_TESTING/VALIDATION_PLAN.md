# Validation plan

**Status: proposed work. No experiments or results are recorded in this repository.** Consumer studies should use food prepared under appropriate professional food safety controls. Do not distribute or store an unvalidated meat prototype at ambient temperature.

## 1. Problem and use occasion

Interview people who regularly make simple pasta, rice, noodles, or potatoes. Ask for the last actual meal, not hypothetical interest. Record how they add protein now, why they skipped it, what they spent, and where they would store an alternative. Include cooks who use bacon bits, jerky, floss, or no topping. Outcome: a ranked list of observed meal occasions and unmet needs.

## 2. Format and distinctiveness

Use photos or professionally safe samples to compare visible flake shapes against powder, jerky pieces, conventional floss, and bacon bits. Blindly ask participants to describe what they see and expect. Outcome: a specific piece and texture target plus the level of differentiation consumers actually perceive.

## 3. In-dish performance

Test at least one hot, moist, and cold application where the SKU is intended to be used. For the pork path, include [the carbonara comparison](../02_PRODUCT_R%26D/PRODUCT.md#carbonara-test-dish). Record appearance, meat recognition, texture over time, flavor, dish satisfaction, and whether users want a second use. Avoid changing both recipe and topping at once.

## 4. Technical feasibility and safety

With a qualified processor and food microbiologist, define a validated lethality step, drying or other stability controls, hygienic handling after that step, packaging, and shelf-life protocol. Test actual products and worst-case pieces and packages. Assess microbiology, water activity, moisture distribution, rancidity, sensory decline, package failure, and post-opening instructions. Apply the EU category and microbiological criteria that fit the final product. See [shelf-life plan](../03_RESEARCH/SHELF_LIFE.md) and [EU regulatory questions](../03_RESEARCH/EU_REGULATION.md).

## 5. Willingness to pay and repeat use

After safe in-home or controlled-use testing, ask participants to buy at stated prices or make a real choice against alternatives. Track uses per pack, replacement of other ingredients, and repeat purchase. A one-time compliment is not demand evidence.

## 6. Pilot decision record

For every test, log date, SKU and batch, recipe, participant criteria, sample size, method, raw observations, failures, and decision. Set pass/fail thresholds **before** seeing results. A pilot can end in reformulation, a narrower use case, or stopping the candidate; record that outcome plainly.
