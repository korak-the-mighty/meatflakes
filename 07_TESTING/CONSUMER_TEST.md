# Consumer test — repeated use in real kitchens

**Proposed only.** Run this with a professionally produced, appropriately safe and labeled test product after the relevant technical and regulatory review. Do not hand households unvalidated home-dried meat or ask them to keep it in a cupboard.

## Design

- Recruit **30–50 households** that actually cook simple meals. Include users and nonusers of packaged toppings; record screening criteria and selection bias.
- Give each household **three small coded packs** from a defined safe pilot, with only necessary use and storage instructions. If testing multiple meats, control pack order. Do not disguise legally required allergen or safety information.
- Observe use for **7–14 days**. A baseline question asks what participants normally do with rice, pasta, noodles, eggs or potatoes; this helps distinguish incremental use from novelty.
- Log each opening and meal: date, dish, approximate amount, who ate it, whether used again, what else they considered, and what remained in the pack. Ask for a photo when practical.
- End with a real choice: buy another pack at a stated price, select a comparator, or decline. Hypothetical liking alone is weak evidence.

## Primary outcomes

The main signal is **unprompted repeat use**: did the household reach for it again, and on what? Also count first-open rate, number of use occasions, pack completion, perceived meatiness, whether it made a dish feel like a meal, texture in actual use and flavor preference. Record the exact denominator for every percentage. Ask why unopened packs stayed unopened.

## Comparisons and bias controls

Where safe and practical, compare the same dish with a commercial bacon topping, pork floss or freshly cooked meat at a similar meat mass. Randomize presentation in blinded taste tasks, but let the home-use period reflect normal use. Do not treat children as a separate product claim; if included in household observation, apply appropriate consent and food safety safeguards.

## Decision

Before recruiting, set a provisional repeat-use and price criterion with the economics model. After the study, report raw counts, missing diaries and negative feedback. If people enjoy a novelty tasting but never use the pouch again, revise the use case or stop. If one application dominates, narrow the launch proposition to it.
