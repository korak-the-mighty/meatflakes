# Product principles

These are design decisions to test, not achieved properties.

1. **Real meat must remain visible.** A customer should recognize distinct pieces in the pack and on food. Powder, fine floss and meat-flavored seasoning are comparison products, not the intended identity.
2. **Make ordinary food useful.** The act should be simple: shake or pour onto rice, pasta, noodles, eggs, potatoes or another everyday base. Test whether it truly makes the meal feel more complete rather than just garnished.
3. **Taste first.** Prototype 001 asks if people want to eat it repeatedly. Do not substitute shelf-life ambition or brand excitement for a good eating experience.
4. **Design for two moments.** Evaluate dry crunch and the texture after contact with hot or moist food. One SKU need not excel at both if distinct versions prove better.
5. **Safety is designed, measured and documented.** Visual dryness is not proof of microbial safety. A qualified partner must validate the process, package, ready-to-eat status and shelf life before sale.
6. **Treat each meat separately.** Chicken, beef and fatty pork or guanciale can differ in flavor, structure, yield and oxidation behavior. Do not extrapolate a result from one species.
7. **Compare against what exists.** Bacon bits, pork floss and jerky set real benchmarks. Different wording is not differentiation; blind use tests must show a meaningful advantage.
8. **Keep claims proportional to evidence.** Do not promise a 12–24+ month life, nutrition benefit, waste reduction, patent protection or value per serving before each is demonstrated.

The working question: **Can a visible real-meat pantry ingredient make a basic dish feel like a satisfying meal with almost no extra preparation?**
