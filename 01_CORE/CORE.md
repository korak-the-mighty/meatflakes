# Core concept

**Concept — keep this definition stable when evaluating variants.**

Meatflakes are **shelf-stable real-meat flakes or toppings** added to everyday foods to turn them into a meal. They look and eat like recognizable pieces of meat, rather than powder, jerky, or conventional meat floss. People can use them on pasta, rice, noodles, potatoes, eggs, salads, and similar everyday dishes. Chicken, beef, and pork or guanciale are candidate meats. The dry product might be crunchy, or might soften or reconstitute when it meets hot or moist food; these are alternative experiences to test, not proven properties. **Carbonara with guanciale flakes** is the clearest demonstration.

## What must be true of the experience

- The consumer can see and identify pieces of real meat in the finished dish.
- A small, convenient addition makes the base food feel more like a complete meal; research must establish whether users actually experience this.
- The unopened product is intended for ambient storage **only after** a safe process, package, and shelf life have been validated.
- The serving method is simple enough to use with food people already make.

## Boundaries

- **Not powder:** meat must remain visually distinct, not merely contribute flavor.
- **Not jerky:** the main use is as a meal ingredient or topping, not a chewable meat snack.
- **Not conventional meat floss:** the desired appearance is discrete flakes or pieces, not a fine, fluffy mass of fibers. Existing floss is still important prior art and a comparison product.
- **Not a claim of invention or exclusivity:** adjacent products and patents exist; see [competitor notes](../03_RESEARCH/COMPETITORS.md) and [prior-art notes](../04_IP_PATENT/PRIOR_ART.md).

## Open design choices

Piece size, thickness, fat level, seasoning, crunch, softening behavior, portion size, pack format, and whether one SKU can work across cold and hot dishes are unresolved. Guanciale brings a particularly difficult fat and oxidation problem; it should not be assumed to use the same process as lean chicken or beef.

## One-sentence test

“Can these visible pieces of shelf-stable real meat make my ordinary pasta, rice, or other base food feel like a satisfying meal with almost no extra preparation?”
